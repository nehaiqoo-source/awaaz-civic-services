India Runs on Voice · Civic services track

Awaaz · आवाज़

Civic services: voice-based grievance filling.
Public services for people who can't fill a form.

Live: www.awaaz.help · Code: github.com/nehaiqoo-source/awaaz-civic-services

The problem

Public services are reached through forms built for people who can read and type.

  • When an old age or widow pension stops, the person has to find the right office or portal and file a complaint. The form is long, usually in English or formal Hindi, and asks for scheme names, offices and reference numbers.
  • India's literacy rate (age 7 and above) is 80.9%, so roughly one person in five aged 7+ cannot read and write. The rate is 77.5% in rural areas and 74.6% for women.
  • Today these people ask a relative, pay someone at a cyber cafe, or give up. The need comes back every time a payment is missed, a ration card changes or a street problem appears.

Source: Periodic Labour Force Survey 2023-24 (NSO, MoSPI), as reported by the Ministry of Education and the press. Figures are for age 7+.

Who we build for

People who can explain their problem out loud, but not on a form.

First users

Older people, widows and retired workers in villages and small towns, using a basic Android phone. Hindi first, with English words mixed in.

The national old age and widow pension schemes alone have ceilings of about 2.21 crore and 0.67 crore beneficiaries.

Why now

India had about 958 million active internet users in 2025; about 548 million (57%) are rural, and rural is growing faster than urban.

In 2024, about 1 in 5 internet users used voice commands.

Sources: PIB, National Social Assistance Programme (Nov 2025); IAMAI and Kantar, Internet in India 2025 (press coverage) and 2024 report.

The solution

Awaaz: say the problem, and it finds the service and fills the form with you.

1. Saathi (साथी)

Say "meri pension teen mahine se nahi aayi". Saathi asks at most two short questions (which kind of pension?) and names the right government service, the papers to keep ready and the official link. It is rule-based and picks only from a fixed, checked list, so it cannot invent a link.

2. Fill any form by voice

On a government form, tap Awaaz. It reads each question aloud, listens, fills the field and reads it back. The person checks the form and presses Submit. Works in the Android app, a Chrome/Firefox extension, or a bookmark.

3. Voice complaint with proof

Speak a local complaint (road, water, drain, light), add a photo and location if you want, and get a tracking ID.

The core flow (shown in the demo)

From one spoken sentence to a filled form.

  1. The person taps the microphone and says the problem in Hindi, English or a mix.
  2. Saathi asks one follow-up aloud with big buttons: central government pension, PF pension, or a state scheme such as old age or widow pension. The answer can be spoken or tapped.
  3. Awaaz shows the right service card, reads it aloud, lists the papers to keep ready and opens the official site.
  4. On the form, Awaaz asks each question aloud, listens, fills the field and says it back for confirmation.
  5. At the end it reads the whole form back. The person presses Submit. Awaaz never submits for them.

If nothing is heard, it asks again; after repeated failures it offers typing. Passwords, OTPs, Aadhaar, PAN and bank numbers are never filled: the person types those.

Why voice comes first

Speaking is the one skill every user already has.

  • The blocker is reading the form and typing in a regional script on a small keyboard, not knowing what the problem is.
  • Every step in Awaaz is a question asked aloud and an answer spoken. Typing exists only as a backup.
  • Answers are read back aloud, so a person who cannot read can still check what was filled.
  • The same voice flow works on a phone app, a browser extension and the website.

Trust and safety by design

Built for people who are easy to mislead.

  • Never submits a form, never fills passwords, OTPs, Aadhaar, PAN or bank numbers.
  • Services come from a closed catalog of government portals; each link was checked, with a fallback to the national portal india.gov.in.
  • Photos are re-encoded on the device, which removes hidden GPS data. Location is shared only when the person taps for it and is rounded to about 11 metres.
  • Photo description runs on the phone (an open model in the browser), so the photo is not sent to an AI service.
  • No account, no ads, no analytics. Microphone only while the person taps and speaks.
  • Strict browser security headers (CSP, no framing) and rate limits on the server.

What is built today

A working prototype, not a mock-up.

  • Website and installable web app: www.awaaz.help (Saathi chat, voice complaint with tracking ID, demo form, 26 help guides in Hindi and English).
  • Chrome and Firefox extension that fills any website form by voice.
  • Android app (signed release bundle ready for Play internal testing).
  • Runs with docker compose up and starts without any API keys.
  • 165 automated tests (voice commands, form filling, safety rules, catalog, chat).
  • Honest status: tested in desktop browsers and an Android emulator. Not yet tested with real users or on real low-end phones; the voice recognition quality for regional accents is the main unknown.

Go-to-market

Reach people through the helpers they already trust.

  • Pilot (first 3 months): one district, about 500 users, working with local helpers, pensioner groups and NGOs. Measure: share of forms finished without someone else typing (target 70%), median time under 10 minutes, under 15% of fields corrected at read-back, 40% return within 60 days.
  • Search: 26 Hindi and English help guides already answer queries like "pension nahi aayi kya kare".
  • Distribution: Android app on Play, the browser extension for cyber cafe operators and helpers, and plain web links shared on WhatsApp.
  • Free for citizens. Funding through grants and CSR first; later a paid white-label version for municipal bodies and NGOs that run help desks.
  • Next features: WhatsApp and phone-call access, more languages, on-device speech recognition, and a way for people to delete their own complaint.
