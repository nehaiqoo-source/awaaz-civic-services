# India Runs on Voice, Round 1: Idea (Civic services track)

Prompt: Describe a voice-first product idea for people in India. Say who has the problem, where and how often it hurts, and why speaking is the main way people would use your product.

## Answer (paste this)

Awaaz is a voice assistant that fills government forms and files civic complaints for people who cannot read or type a long form.

**Who has the problem.** First-generation internet users, older people, people with low literacy, and anyone whose language is not the one the form is written in. Think of a widow whose pension has stopped, a farmer whose PM-KISAN payment has not come, a family waiting on a ration card, or a street with a pothole nobody has fixed.

**Where and how often it hurts.** Every time they need a public service. Most government forms and portals are in English or formal Hindi, long, and built for typing on a keyboard. In practice these people ask a relative or pay someone at a cyber cafe to fill the form, or they give up. A pension, a ration card or a road repair is not a one-time need: it comes back every year, and often every few months.

**Why speaking is the main way in.** Most people who struggle with forms can still say clearly what the problem is, in their own language. Typing in a regional script on a small phone keyboard is the hard part, and so is understanding what a field wants. Awaaz asks each question aloud, listens to the answer in Hindi, English or another Indian language, and fills the field. Typing is only the backup.

**What it does today.**
- Saathi: say your problem ("pension nahi aayi", "gali mein gaddha hai") and it names the right government service, what to keep ready, and the official link. It asks a short follow-up when the answer is unclear.
- Fill any form by voice, through a phone app, a browser extension or a bookmark. The person checks the form and presses Submit; Awaaz never submits for them and never fills passwords, OTPs, Aadhaar, PAN or bank numbers.
- Voice complaint with an optional photo and location, and a tracking ID.

**Safety by design.** Services come from a fixed, checked list of government portals, so the assistant cannot invent a link. Photos lose hidden location data before upload. Location is shared only when the person taps for it.
